# Original comedy, scripts and planning

## Provisional @DailyRaaz brief

The user requested original comedy for this handle. Until supplied with real samples,
assume only: creator-led situational comedy, solo production possible, ordinary props,
and a complete joke per post. English examples below are a starting point, not an
inference about preferred language. Ask before treating an invented character or voice
as the creator's established identity. Avoid inferred ethnicity, nationality or accent.

Develop ideas from an everyday friction + a character's irrational rule + escalation +
a reversal. Trends may supply a structure, not another creator's dialogue, catchphrase,
character, shot sequence or punchline. Do a similarity pass against supplied references;
flag recognizable overlap and rewrite the mechanism. Do not promise global novelty.
If asked for a current trend, verify dated sources or explain that no live research was
available. Do not call a stock format trending from memory.

Idea cards should contain premise, first visible action, two possible opening lines,
escalation, punchline, props/performers, rough duration and a specific originality note.
Rank by fit with the creator's voice, strength of reversal and production effort. A
ranking is editorial, not a virality score. Avoid humiliating strangers, dangerous
pranks, stereotypes about protected groups and invented claims about real people.

## Five original starter concepts

1. **Customer service for your own brain.** The creator calls their brain about a missing
   thought. Opening A: “I'd like to report a thought missing.” Opening B: “My brain has
   put me on hold.” Escalation: the operator requests proof they entered the room for a
   reason. Payoff: the only recovered thought is the hold music. Solo split character;
   phone prop; about 25 seconds. Mechanism: bureaucracy applied to forgetfulness.
2. **The snack's exit interview.** A person interviews the final biscuit before eating it.
   Opening A: “Why are you leaving the packet?” Opening B: “Your last biscuit has feedback.”
   Escalation: the biscuit cites poor job security. Payoff: the interviewer offers a
   permanent position, then reads “in my stomach.” One prop; about 20 seconds. Mechanism:
   an ordinary snack becomes an employee with a grievance.
3. **Loading-screen roommate.** A person freezes whenever asked to help. Opening A:
   “My roommate only buffers during chores.” Opening B: “Watch the connection drop.”
   Escalation: repeated requests freeze them, but a snack offer gets an instant response.
   Payoff: the frozen person says, “Chores require the premium plan.” Solo role swap;
   one room; about 25 seconds. Mechanism: selective effort expressed as software tiers.
4. **Tomorrow's calendar files a complaint.** The creator sends every task to tomorrow.
   Opening A: “Tomorrow asked for a meeting.” Opening B: “My calendar has unionized.”
   Escalation: tomorrow reads out absurdly overlapping appointments. Payoff: tomorrow
   moves the meeting to today and quits. Paper calendar; about 30 seconds. Mechanism:
   procrastination gets a negotiating opponent.
5. **The receipt becomes a life coach.** A shopper studies a long receipt for one item.
   Opening A: “I bought toothpaste and got a novel.” Opening B: “My receipt staged an
   intervention.” Escalation: coupons become advice about other unnecessary purchases.
   Payoff: a coupon offers “one free walk out of the shop.” Paper strip; about 20 seconds.
   Mechanism: shopping paperwork judges the shopper's habits; no real brand claim.

These premises need voice adaptation and a reference check before use. Do not represent
them as observed successful content, or recycle all five indefinitely as fresh ideas.

## Worked draft: loading-screen roommate (about 25 seconds)

Alternative openings: “My roommate only buffers during chores” establishes the pattern;
“Watch the connection drop” adds a mystery but needs an immediately visible task.
Choose the first for clarity. Start in action, no logo or introduction.

| Time | Speech | Action / sound | On-screen text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3s | A: “My roommate only buffers during chores.” | A points at B beside a full laundry basket. | Connection test |
| 3–7s | A: “Can you fold these?” | B freezes halfway through a nod. Add an original soft loading hum. | Buffering… |
| 7–11s | A: “Take the bins out?” | B's frozen smile becomes painfully determined. | Still buffering… |
| 11–15s | A: “Want the last slice?” B: “Yes.” | Instant turn and outstretched hand; hard cut. | Connected |
| 15–20s | A: “So you CAN hear me?” | B slowly looks at the basket, then directly at A. | Service restored? |
| 20–25s | B: “Chores require the premium plan.” | B freezes again; A holds up an obviously homemade invoice. Cut on reaction. | Upgrade declined |

Solo shooting: lock the phone, change one wardrobe item, leave clean gaps for reactions.
The timing includes pauses; confirm by reading and performing it, then cut to the joke's
actual rhythm. Keep captions legible and away from interface overlays; preview on a
phone. Use original or properly licensed sound. Do not require a CTA after a punchline;
if one helps, offer one natural prompt rather than engagement bait. Caption draft:
“Full signal for snacks.” Suggested topic tags should be relevant, not a guarantee of reach.
